---
title: If AI can do everything, what's left?
description: Most people never even think to ask AI. If the skill that matters is believing everything digital is possible and finding the way, what sets you apart once everyone can build like that?
date: 2026-11-02
---

Most people don't hold back from asking AI because they think it can't do the job. They never even think to ask.

That's why I believe the core skill of the AI era is an attitude, not a technology. Specifically, this one:

> AI can do it all. If it doesn't work, I just haven't found the way yet.

## I didn't think to ask at first either

The idea that you could just have AI do it doesn't occur to people. The old ways are so familiar that our thinking has set around them.

The [understanding notes](/en/blog/building-understanding-notes/) for the Korean electrical engineer exam were like that. At first it never crossed my mind, and I was planning to organize the material the old, familiar way. Then the idea suddenly struck me, and I gave it a try. I made very detailed requests of many kinds, and it produced a remarkable new textbook.

Everything I need is possible in the digital world. Even so, most people are still non-AI people.

## But it takes you literally

AI is still like a naive new hire, of course. It won't read your intent and do the sensible thing without being told, which is what Koreans call *aljalttakkalsen*. It doesn't go beyond the instructions. It interprets and carries them out exactly as written.

So you have to direct the work well to get what you want. Those notes became a new textbook only after their instructions changed in a big way four times on the first day. I wrote up how I direct the work in [another post](/en/blog/specs-then-ai/).

I suspect this is because it has no shared experience with or memory of the person giving the instructions. Once it has seen enough of someone's patterns, I think it will get better at telling what a given phrase really means. This is my guess.

## The other side of "it can do everything"

If it can do everything for me, it can do everything for anyone else. Anything can be copied, so standing out is hard. Software on its own, especially, will find it harder and harder to stand out.

## What's left

What matters are the analog things that can't be copied digitally:

- What people think of you
- Customer support
- Work done offline

Digital things are fine too, as long as they can't be copied. Private digital assets, meaning data, are like that.

To build, I believe AI can do it all and look for the way. To stand out, I look where that same effort can't copy. It's still a prediction, but it's how I see the AI era today.
